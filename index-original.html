I Was in Love

Bluias

For a long time, I kept a train ticket in the pocket of a coat I hardly wore. It was from the first Saturday I came to see you. I had taken the wrong exit at the station and called you from outside a flower shop, pretending I knew where I was. You found me before I finished explaining.

You brought two coffees. By the time we remembered to drink them, they were cold. We had walked past your street twice because neither of us wanted to interrupt the conversation. When I finally caught my train home, you stayed on the platform until I could no longer see you.

After that, Saturdays became something I planned the rest of my week around. I would put a book in my bag, although I rarely read on the journey. Mostly I watched the towns pass and wondered what we would do that afternoon. Even a trip to buy groceries felt worth the distance.

When you told me about the job, I helped you choose the photograph for your application. We celebrated when you got it. Then we began making plans around a different timetable, and later around different time zones. I would wake up to a message about a day you had already finished. There was always something I wanted to tell you that felt too small to write down.

Our last call was gentle. Afterwards, I washed the cup beside my laptop and went to bed much earlier than usual. For weeks I kept reaching for my phone on Friday evenings. Once, I opened the train app and almost bought the familiar ticket before remembering there was no visit to arrange.

I found the old ticket while looking for my keys this morning. The print had faded, but I could still read the date. I put it inside the book I used to carry, then went out. At the café, I ordered one coffee and stayed long enough to finish it while it was hot.

Blue Heels

Bluias

Some outfits begin with the dress. This one began with the shoes. A pair of blue heels changed the whole mood before anything else had a chance to compete.

Blue can feel calm from a distance, but on a sharp heel it becomes something else: confident, electric and impossible to ignore. The color carried the look without needing to shout.

The best part was the contrast. Clean lines above, a stronger statement below. Every step gave the outfit movement, and every angle made the blue feel different.

That is what we love about one unforgettable detail. You do not need to overload a look when one choice already has enough personality.

Blue heels are not just an accessory here. They are the point of view: polished, playful and a little fearless.

Love Hurts

By Kendra Bluia

I used to think heartbreak announced itself loudly. I imagined slammed doors, last messages and a clean ending you could point to. What I learned instead is that love can hurt quietly. Sometimes nothing dramatic happens at all. Two people simply stop meeting each other in the same place.

At first I kept searching for the exact moment things changed. I replayed conversations, reread old words and tried to decide which version of us had been real. The difficult answer was that all of them were. Love can be sincere and still fail. A beautiful memory does not become false just because it does not become a future.

The hardest part was letting go of the person I had become inside that relationship. I had built small routines around being expected, understood and chosen. When that disappeared, even ordinary days felt unfamiliar. I confused missing the routine with needing the relationship back.

Eventually I stopped asking whether I had been enough. That question kept me trapped because it treated love like an exam with a score. Two people can care deeply and still be wrong for each other in the life they are actually living. No amount of effort can turn incompatibility into peace.

Healing did not arrive as one brave decision. It came in smaller choices: not checking, not reopening a conversation that had already ended, going somewhere new, laughing without feeling guilty, and letting a day pass without measuring it against the past.

I still believe in love. I just believe in it differently now. Love should not require you to disappear inside it. If it hurts, the pain can teach you where your boundaries were missing and what kind of tenderness you need next time. The ending did not make me colder. It made me clearer.
